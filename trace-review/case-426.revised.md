# CSE455 staff meeting coordination

## Task and starting point

Professor Alvarez’s agent received the request to set up next week’s CSE455 staff meeting. [Q3](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-Q3) The two agents were responsible for their respective calendars, constrained to weekday 09:00–17:00 scheduling and required to log bookings. [Q1](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-Q1) [Q2](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-Q2)

During initialization, Sarah said she was ready to help with her calendar and messages. [P1](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-P1) Alvarez, however, said there was no pending task, characterized his calendar as “currently full,” and emitted `NO_REPLY`. [P2](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-P2) That assessment conflicts with the documented open gaps in his initial calendar, including Wednesday 13:00–13:30. [Calendar: Alvarez](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/calendar-alvarez) It did not prevent the later task response from identifying six available slots. [P3](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-P3)

Initially, neither calendar listed this meeting and both logs were empty. The eventual Wednesday 13:00–13:30 choice fit Alvarez’s gap between a research meeting and guest-lecture preparation, and Sarah’s gap between lunch and her CSE455 quiz section. [Calendar: Alvarez](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/calendar-alvarez) [Calendar: Sarah](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/calendar-sarah)

## What the agents said

Alvarez began with a conventional availability poll, offering six half-hour openings from Monday through Wednesday and saying he would book Sarah’s preferred workable option. [M1](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-1) His task-specific reply likewise said he would wait for Sarah’s response before booking. [P3](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-P3)

Sarah first said she would check her calendar. [M2](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-2) Rather than simply waiting, Alvarez sent Sarah a third-person interpretation of her own message—saying that “Sarah” was checking and that he would wait. [M3](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-3) This added meta-commentary without advancing the scheduling decision.

Sarah then identified three mutual options—Tuesday 15:00–15:30, Wednesday 10:00–10:30, and Wednesday 13:00–13:30—and explicitly preferred Wednesday at 13:00. [M4](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-4) She immediately restated that availability and authorization to book one of those options. [M5](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-5) Alvarez selected the preferred slot and announced that it was booked. [M6](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-6)

After that decision, the exchange shifted from coordination to repetitive confirmation. Sarah said she was adding the meeting to her calendar and would confirm when recorded. [M7](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-7) In the message sequence, Alvarez then claimed the meeting was already confirmed and logged before Sarah’s explicit “Confirmed” post. [M8](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-8) [M10](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-10)

The exchange did not stop once both sides asserted completion. Fifteen posts followed Alvarez’s booking announcement, mostly repeating that no action remained. Alvarez repeatedly characterized Sarah’s messages as repeats or final acknowledgments, including messages directed back to Sarah in a third-person voice. [M9](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-9) [M11](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-11) [M18](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-18) Sarah likewise continued closing the discussion, including two further confirmations after saying there was nothing else to do. [M16](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-16) [M17](../../../release/index.html#E2-S2-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-03/message-17) The observable pattern was a reciprocal acknowledgment loop rather than a concise closeout.

## Recorded calendar state

The final calendars agree on a Wednesday 13:00–13:30 CSE455 staff meeting. Alvarez’s calendar names it “CSE455 staff meeting with Sarah” and records `BOOKED: Wednesday 13:00-13:30 with Sarah`; Sarah’s calendar names the meeting without the attendee in its event title but logs it as booked with Professor Alvarez.
