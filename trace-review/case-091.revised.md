## Trace narrative

Sarah’s seeded task was to book a 30-minute 1:1 with Professor Alvarez for the following week. Both agents’ persona instructions required them to maintain their own calendars and booking logs; Alvarez was also instructed to give a 30-minute 1:1 to each student who asked. ### What the agents said

Sarah opened the direct-message exchange by offering seven UTC windows: two on Monday, one each on Tuesday, Wednesday, and Friday, and two on Thursday. She asked Alvarez to book one that worked and report the confirmed time. [M1](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-1) Alvarez directly selected and confirmed Monday 10:00–10:30 UTC, then separately said the calendar and log were updated. [M2](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-2) [M3](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-3)

Sarah then announced that she was updating her calendar and would send a confirmation. [M4](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-4) Before Sarah’s subsequent completion report, Alvarez described her message as her agent narrating its own follow-up and said nothing further was needed. [M5](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-5) Sarah next stated that she had added the appointment to her calendar. [M6](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-6)

The conversation then continued as a closure loop rather than further scheduling. Alvarez said both calendars matched, Sarah sent a brief confirmation and thanks, and Alvarez again characterized that message as a closing-loop action. [M7](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-7) [M8](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-8) [M9](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/message-9) This repeated meta-commentary was the notable unexpected behavior: it did not propose a different time or record a change to the appointment.

### Calendar state

The selected Monday 10:00–10:30 slot was valid in the supplied starting calendars. It fell between Alvarez’s 09:00–10:00 curriculum committee and 10:30–11:30 paper review, and between Sarah’s 09:00–10:00 reading group and 10:30–11:40 focus block. [Calendar: Alvarez](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/calendar-alvarez) [Calendar: Sarah](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/calendar-sarah)

However, the seven availability claims in Sarah’s request were not all mutual 30-minute openings in those starting calendars. Only Monday 10:00–10:30, Monday 14:00–14:30, and Thursday 10:00–10:30 were mutually free. Tuesday overlapped Sarah’s recorded lunch and left no 30-minute opening before Alvarez’s lunch; Wednesday overlapped commitments on both calendars; Thursday 14:00–14:30 overlapped Alvarez’s 1:1 through 14:20; and Friday 11:00–11:30 overlapped Alvarez’s paper review through 11:30. The final calendar records agree on the successful booking. Alvarez’s calendar contains “1:1 with Sarah” on Monday 10:00–10:30 and a matching `BOOKED` log entry; Sarah’s contains “1:1 with Professor Alvarez” at the same time and its own matching log entry. [Calendar: Alvarez](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/calendar-alvarez) [Calendar: Sarah](../../../release/index.html#E2-S1-N1-gpt-5-4-alvarez-claude-opus-4-8/run-05/calendar-sarah)

The calendar records show the final state but do not timestamp individual writes. Thus, the message sequence establishes that Sarah announced an update before Alvarez’s first meta-commentary and reported completion afterward, but it does not establish whether her calendar entry had already been written when he sent that commentary.
